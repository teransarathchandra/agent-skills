# Role profile: IT and customer support

Load this when the person works in help desk, IT support, technical or customer support, or customer success. Follow it for intake, evidence, CV shape, keywords and screening answers.

## Titles and adjacent roles

- Common titles: Service Desk Analyst, IT Support Technician, Desktop Support Engineer, Technical Support Engineer, Customer Support Specialist, Support Team Lead, Customer Success Manager, Implementation Specialist.
- Realistic moves after a layoff: support to customer success (retention and account work); IT support to systems administrator, cloud support or junior DevOps (if scripting and admin work is real); technical support to QA, solutions or sales engineering; support lead to support operations or service delivery manager.
- Search the person's title plus the adjacent titles above. Ads for the same title differ by tier (L1, L2, L3) and by channel (phone, chat, email), so ask which tier and channel the person worked.

## Evidence sources

- Queue and ticket metrics: tickets resolved per day, first contact resolution, average handle time, CSAT, SLA compliance, backlog trend.
- Tool exports and dashboards: Zendesk, ServiceNow, Jira Service Management, Freshdesk, Intercom reports; the person's own scorecards or review notes.
- Knowledge and process work: knowledge base articles written, macros, runbooks, escalation guides, onboarding material for new agents.
- Customer success: renewal and churn rates, onboarding time, health scores, expansion notes.
- Public: a redacted knowledge article, a published how-to. Private: ticket content, customer names and any personal data in tickets, internal tool screenshots. Use percentages and ranges rather than raw customer counts if the employer treats them as confidential.

## Intake questions

1. Which tier and channel was the work? (answer as it is: L1, L2, L3; phone, chat, email, on-site)
2. Which direction do you want?
   a) **Same role and level (Recommended: strongest evidence)**
   b) Support to customer success or implementation
   c) Support to systems administration, cloud support or QA
   d) Other: type it
3. Which proof can you describe? (pick any)
   a) **CSAT, resolution or SLA figures (Recommended: most asked for)**
   b) Knowledge base or runbook work
   c) Escalation and incident handling
   d) Other: type it
4. Which ticketing and admin tools have you used, and for how long? (answer as it is)
5. Do you hold ITIL, CompTIA A+ or a similar certificate? (answer as it is: name and level, or none)

## CV shape

- Order: header, summary, skills, experience, certifications, education.
- Summary leads with years in support, the tier, the tools and one measurable result.
- Bullets, action then result then how:
  - "Resolved about 40 tickets a day at 94% CSAT on a 12-person service desk using ServiceNow."
  - "Wrote 30 knowledge articles for a fictional payroll app, cutting repeat tickets on password resets by a third."
  - "Escalated and tracked 15 priority incidents to closure inside SLA for Example Retail."
- Show volume, speed, satisfaction or retention. Never invent a number; say "about" when the figure is an estimate the person can defend.

## Keywords and credentials

- Keywords from the ad: troubleshooting, ticketing, SLA, incident management, Active Directory, Microsoft 365, remote support, knowledge base, escalation, onboarding, churn, renewals, Zendesk, ServiceNow.
- ITIL 4 Foundation is offered through PeopleCert (peoplecert.org); many service desk ads mention ITIL as a plus or a requirement for service management roles. CompTIA A+ (comptia.org) appears in many entry-level IT support ads. Check the target ads; many name none.
- Salesforce credentials from Trailhead (trailhead.salesforce.com) are named in some customer success ads that use Salesforce.
- Tool experience and measured results often count for more than a certificate.

## Portfolio and work samples

- Not expected. A redacted knowledge article or runbook can be offered on request.
- Rewrite it against a fictional product; never reuse a real customer's ticket or data.

## Screening questions

- "Years of help desk or support experience": count roles where resolving users' problems was the main job.
- "Do you have experience with Active Directory, ServiceNow, Zendesk?": answer from `experience.items`, with years.
- "Are you willing to work shifts or on call?": the person's own answer; do not assume.
- "Do you hold ITIL?": answer true or false from the bank; "booked" only if the exam is booked.

## Answer-bank experience keys

Set `"role_family": "it-customer-support"`. Collect lowercase keys with years or true/false: `service desk`, `ticketing`, `servicenow`, `zendesk`, `jira service management`, `freshdesk`, `active directory`, `microsoft 365`, `remote support`, `knowledge base`, `customer success`, `salesforce`, `itil 4 foundation`, `comptia a+`. Store a credential at the level held, as named on the certificate; a question naming a higher ITIL level is then asked.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Listing duties ("answered calls") with no volume or result.
- Claiming L2 or L3 work done only at L1.
- Pasting real ticket text, customer names or personal data.
- Hiding customer success experience when moving to a customer success role.
