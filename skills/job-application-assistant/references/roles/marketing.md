# Role profile: marketing

Load this when the person works in digital, content, product or growth marketing. Follow it for intake, evidence, CV shape, keywords and screening answers.

## Titles and adjacent roles

- Common titles: Digital Marketing Specialist, Content Marketer, SEO Specialist, Paid Media or PPC Specialist, Email Marketing Manager, Social Media Manager, Product Marketing Manager, Growth Marketer, Marketing Operations Specialist.
- Realistic moves after a layoff: content to product marketing or technical writing; paid media to growth or marketing operations; marketing generalist to a specialist channel; social media to community or brand; marketing operations to revenue operations.
- Search the person's title plus adjacent ones. Ads vary by channel, so ask which channels and budgets the person actually ran.

## Evidence sources

- Campaign results: traffic, leads, conversion rate, cost per lead, return on ad spend, email open and click rates, organic ranking and growth.
- Platform reports: Google Analytics, Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager, HubSpot, Mailchimp, Search Console. Use percentages or ranges.
- Work product: published articles, landing pages, campaigns, newsletters, product launches, brand guidelines.
- Public: published work with the person's byline, a portfolio page, a case study using fictional or approved numbers. Private: client and employer budgets, unreleased campaigns, customer lists, platform logins and screenshots showing account IDs. Agency work may be under client NDA; ask before naming a client.

## Intake questions

1. Which channels were the main work? (answer as it is: SEO, paid, content, email, social, product marketing, analytics)
2. Which direction do you want?
   a) **Same role and level (Recommended: strongest evidence)**
   b) Move to a related specialty (for example content to product marketing)
   c) Marketing generalist to marketing operations or growth
   d) Other: type it
3. Which results can you share? (pick any)
   a) **Percent change in leads, traffic or conversion (Recommended: often asked for)**
   b) Return on ad spend or cost per lead
   c) Launches and published work
   d) Other: type it
4. Do you have a portfolio or published work you can link? (answer as it is: links, or none)
5. Which tools and certifications do you have? (answer as it is: names, or none)

## CV shape

- Order: header, summary, skills, experience, projects (selected campaigns, each with a link), certifications, education.
- Summary leads with years, the channels, the sector and one result.
- Bullets, action then result then how:
  - "Grew organic sessions 65% in 12 months for a fictional travel site through topic clusters and refreshed pages."
  - "Ran paid search across 4 markets, lowering cost per lead by 22% with new ad structure and negative keywords."
  - "Launched a product feature to 8,000 subscribers by email, reaching a 41% open rate."
- Show the change and the time frame, not only the activity. Never invent a number.

## Keywords and credentials

- Keywords from the ad: SEO, SEM, PPC, content strategy, Google Analytics, GA4, A/B testing, conversion rate optimisation, email marketing, marketing automation, HubSpot, CRM, lead generation, copywriting, KPIs.
- Google Skillshop (skillshop.withgoogle.com) hosts Google Ads and Google Analytics certifications; HubSpot Academy (academy.hubspot.com) offers HubSpot certifications; CIM (cim.co.uk) offers qualifications often named in UK ads. Results and samples usually count for more than certificates; check the target ads.

## Portfolio and work samples

- Often expected for content, SEO, social and product marketing; less so for paid media.
- Good samples: 3 to 5 pieces with a one-line goal and result each, public links, nothing under NDA. Rebuild private work as a fictional example.

## Screening questions

- "Years of marketing experience": count work in the named channel.
- "Experience with Google Ads, GA4, HubSpot": answer from `experience.items`.
- "Budget you managed": the person's own figure; offer a band if confidential and say so.
- "Link to your portfolio": only real links the person gives.
- "Hold Google Ads certification": true or false; "expired" if it has lapsed.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Listing vanity numbers (impressions) with no business result.
- Claiming credit for team results without saying the person's part.
- Showing client work or budgets covered by an NDA.
- Listing every tool ever opened; keep the ones the person can discuss.
