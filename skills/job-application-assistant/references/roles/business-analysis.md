# Role profile: business and systems analysis

Load this when the person is a business analyst or systems analyst. Follow it for intake, evidence, CV shape, keywords and screening answers.

## Titles and adjacent roles

- Common titles: Business Analyst, Systems Analyst, Functional Analyst, Technical Business Analyst, Business Systems Analyst, Process Analyst, Requirements Analyst, Product Analyst.
- Realistic moves after a layoff: BA to product owner (requirements and stakeholder work carries over); BA to product manager, project manager or Scrum Master; BA to data analyst (if SQL and reporting are real); BA to UX researcher or QA lead in some teams; domain BA (banking, insurance, health) to the same domain with a new title.
- Search the person's title plus "analyst", "requirements" and "product owner". Product owner ads often reward the backlog and acceptance-criteria evidence a BA already has.

## Evidence sources

- Requirement documents: BRDs, user stories with acceptance criteria, use cases, traceability matrices.
- Process and data artefacts: BPMN or flow maps, as-is and to-be models, data mappings, wireframes.
- Workshop and delivery records: workshop notes, sign-offs, UAT results, defects traced to requirements.
- Tool exports: Jira and Confluence, Azure DevOps, Visio or Lucidchart files.
- Public: a redacted sample set, a process-mapping write-up. Private under an NDA: real requirements, client process names, data fields. Replace names with generic stand-ins.

## Intake questions

1. Which kind of analysis is the main strength? (answer as it is: requirements, process improvement, data, systems integration, domain expertise)
2. Which direction do you want?
   a) **Same role and level (Recommended: strongest evidence)**
   b) BA to product owner or product manager
   c) BA to project, delivery or data analyst roles
   d) Other: type it
3. Which artefacts can you describe or show? (pick any)
   a) **User stories and acceptance criteria (Recommended: most asked for)**
   b) Process maps and data mappings
   c) UAT and sign-off records
   d) Other: type it
4. Do you hold a BA certificate such as ECBA, CCBA or CBAP? (answer as it is: name and level, or none)
5. Which domain have you worked in, and for how long? (answer as it is)

## CV shape

- Order: header, summary, skills, experience, certifications, education.
- Summary leads with years in analysis, the domain, the methods (agile, waterfall) and one delivered result.
- Bullets, action then result then how:
  - "Wrote 85 user stories with acceptance criteria for a loan onboarding rebuild, cutting rework in UAT by a quarter at Example Bank."
  - "Mapped 14 claim-handling processes in BPMN, exposing 3 manual handoffs that were removed."
  - "Facilitated 10 stakeholder workshops across 4 departments to agree one scope."
- Show requirements delivered, decisions influenced or time saved. Never invent a number.

## Keywords and credentials

- Keywords from the ad: requirements gathering, user stories, acceptance criteria, BPMN, UML, stakeholder management, UAT, gap analysis, SQL, Jira, Confluence, Agile, process improvement.
- IIBA (iiba.org) lists ECBA (entry), CCBA (experienced) and CBAP (advanced), plus Agile Analysis Certification and CPOA. CBAP or a similar certificate is often named as a plus, not always; check the target ads.
- Domain knowledge and delivered requirements often count for more than a certificate.

## Portfolio and work samples

- Not usually expected. A redacted user-story set, process map or requirements outline can be offered if asked.
- Rebuild it from a fictional or public scenario; never reuse a client's real document.

## Screening questions

- "Years as a business analyst": count roles where analysis was the main job.
- "Do you write user stories?": yes only with real examples from the bank.
- "Which tools: Jira, Visio, SQL?": answer from `experience.items`.
- "Do you hold CBAP or CCBA?": answer from the bank, true or false; "in progress" only if booked.

## Answer-bank experience keys

Collect lowercase keys with years or true/false: `business analysis`, `requirements gathering`, `user stories`, `bpmn`, `uml`, `process mapping`, `uat`, `sql`, `jira`, `confluence`, `visio`, `lucidchart`, `agile`, `data mapping`, `ecba`, `ccba`, `cbap`.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Listing "gathered requirements" with no delivered result.
- Calling a project coordinator or tester role analysis.
- Leaving out the domain, which BA ads often rank highly.
- Pasting real requirement text or process names under an NDA.
