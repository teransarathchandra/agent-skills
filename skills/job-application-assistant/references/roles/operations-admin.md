# Role profile: operations and administration

Load this when the person works in operations, office or executive administration, coordination or procurement. Follow it for intake, evidence, CV shape, keywords and screening answers.

## Titles and adjacent roles

- Common titles: Operations Coordinator, Operations Manager, Office Manager, Administrative Assistant, Executive Assistant, Personal Assistant, Business Support Officer, Procurement Officer, Buyer, Supply Chain Coordinator, Facilities Coordinator.
- Realistic moves after a layoff: administrator to operations coordinator; executive assistant to chief of staff, project coordination or office management; operations to project or programme management; procurement to supply chain or vendor management; office manager to HR administration or people operations.
- Search the person's title plus adjacent ones. Titles are used loosely across employers, so read the duties in the ad rather than relying on the title.

## Evidence sources

- Process and cost work: processes documented or improved, time saved, costs reduced, suppliers renegotiated, purchase orders handled.
- Scale: people supported, executives or teams served, events organised, travel and meetings coordinated, budgets administered (in bands).
- Systems: ERP or procurement tools, Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, calendar and expense tools, project and ticketing tools.
- Public: a redacted checklist or process map built on a fictional scenario. Private: executives' calendars, travel and personal details, contracts, supplier pricing, salaries, confidential board or HR matters. Executive assistants hold especially confidential information; describe the role by scope, never by content.

## Intake questions

1. Which kind of work was the main job? (answer as it is: administration, executive support, operations coordination, procurement, facilities, supply chain)
2. Which direction do you want?
   a) **Same role and level (Recommended: strongest evidence)**
   b) Admin or EA to operations, coordination or project roles
   c) Operations to procurement, supply chain or project management
   d) Other: type it
3. Which results can you share? (pick any)
   a) **Time or cost saved, or process improved (Recommended: most asked for)**
   b) Scale: people, events, orders or suppliers handled
   c) Systems introduced or owned
   d) Other: type it
4. Which tools have you used? (answer as it is: names and years)
5. Do you hold a procurement, administration or project certificate? (answer as it is: name, or none)

## CV shape

- Order: header, summary, skills, experience, certifications, education.
- Summary leads with years, the scope (people, teams or spend band), the tools and one result.
- Bullets, action then result then how:
  - "Supported 3 directors and a 40-person team at a fictional logistics firm, coordinating about 25 meetings a week and quarterly offsites."
  - "Moved supplier ordering to a shared tracker, cutting order errors by 30%."
  - "Negotiated renewals with 12 vendors, saving about 9% a year."
- Show scale, accuracy and savings. Never invent a number.

## Keywords and credentials

- Keywords from the ad: calendar management, travel coordination, minute-taking, stakeholder communication, vendor management, purchase orders, procurement, inventory, process improvement, Microsoft 365, ERP, SAP, budgeting, confidentiality, project coordination.
- CIPS (cips.org) offers procurement qualifications and MCIPS membership, often named in UK and international procurement ads. Admin and operations ads may name no certificate and value tools and references; check the target ads.

## Portfolio and work samples

- Not expected. Procurement and operations ads sometimes ask for a case example or Excel test.
- Rebuild any sample from a fictional scenario; never reuse real supplier or executive information.

## Screening questions

- "Years of administrative or operations experience": count roles where the work was the main job.
- "Experience supporting senior executives": yes only if true; give the level, not names.
- "Experience with SAP, Microsoft 365, Concur": answer from `experience.items`.
- "Comfortable with confidential information": a truthful yes with context, never with specifics.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Duties-only bullets such as "answered phones and filed documents".
- Revealing executives' names, travel or confidential matters.
- Understating scope; support for many people or teams belongs on the CV.
- Naming suppliers or contract values the employer treats as private.
