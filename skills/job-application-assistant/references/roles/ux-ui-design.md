# Role profile: UX, UI and product design

Load this when the person designs or researches products: UX, UI, product and interaction design, UX research. Follow it for intake, evidence, CV shape, keywords and screening answers.

## Titles and adjacent roles

- Common titles: UX Designer, UI Designer, Product Designer, Interaction Designer, UX Researcher, Visual Designer, Design Systems Designer, Content Designer.
- Realistic moves after a layoff: UI to product designer; UX designer to UX researcher (or back); designer to product owner or product manager; visual designer to design systems or front-end design roles; agency to in-house design.
- Search the person's title plus "product designer", "UX" and "interaction designer". Ads often use the titles loosely.

## Evidence sources

- Portfolio and case studies (the main evidence), Figma files, prototypes, design system pages.
- Research artefacts: interview guides, synthesis boards, usability test findings.
- Outcome records: shipped screens, analytics changes (conversion, task success), engineer or product manager praise.
- Public: shipped products anyone can open, personal projects, talks, articles. Private under an NDA: unreleased screens, real user data, internal metrics, research participants. See the redaction rules below.

## Intake questions

1. Which design work is the main strength? (answer as it is: research, interaction, visual or UI, design systems, content)
2. Which direction do you want?
   a) **Same role and level (Recommended: strongest evidence)**
   b) UI to UX or product designer, or UX to research
   c) Design to product owner or product manager
   d) Other: type it
3. Do you have a portfolio today?
   a) **Yes, a site or link (Recommended: reviewers expect it)**
   b) Files only, so build a PDF
   c) Mostly NDA work, so build redacted case studies
   d) Other: type it
4. Which two projects show your best thinking? (answer as it is; one case study each)
5. Do you hold a UX certificate, such as the NN/g UX Certification? (answer as it is: name, or none)

## CV shape

- Order: header with portfolio link, summary, skills, experience, education, certifications. One page for early career, two at most.
- Summary leads with years, the specialism (research, product, UI), product type and the outcome.
- Bullets, problem then action then result:
  - "Redesigned checkout for a 400k-user app after 12 usability tests, lifting completion from 61% to 72% at Example Travel."
  - "Built a 60-component design system in Figma adopted by 5 product teams, cutting handoff questions."
  - "Ran 18 customer interviews and shared findings that changed the Q3 roadmap."
- Link the portfolio in the header. Never invent a metric.

## Keywords and credentials

- Keywords from the ad: user research, wireframes, prototyping, usability testing, design systems, interaction design, accessibility, WCAG, Figma, information architecture, design thinking, user flows.
- Design hiring usually weighs the portfolio over any certificate. The NN/g UX Certification (nngroup.com) is a vendor certificate earned by passing courses with exams; check the target ads before leading with it. Do not present a certificate as a substitute for case studies.

## Portfolio and work samples

- Expected for almost all design roles; reviewers often open the link before reading the CV.
- A good case study shows: the problem and the person's role, the team and constraints, the research or evidence, options explored (sketches, rejected ideas), the decision and why, the final design, and the result with honest numbers or lessons. Two to four strong studies beat ten thin ones.
- NDA-safe redaction: ask the employer where possible; blur or replace logos, names, data and copy; change numbers into percentages or ranges; show process more than final screens; add a note that details are altered. Never show real user data or unreleased features.
- Link or PDF: a link (own site, Notion, Behance) is the default and is easy to update; add a password if needed and put it in the application. A PDF suits uploads that accept only files, and NDA-heavy work sent privately. Keep a PDF under about 10 MB and check the link opens logged out.
- No client work at all: use a self-initiated project, a redesign of a public product with a clear label, or a volunteer project.

## Screening questions

- "Link to your portfolio": give the live link; do not link private files.
- "Years of UX experience": count design and research roles; UI-only years may be asked separately.
- "Which tools: Figma, Sketch, Adobe XD?": answer from `experience.items`.
- "Experience with user research or accessibility?": answer from real projects in the bank.

## Answer-bank experience keys

Collect lowercase keys with years or true/false: `ux design`, `ui design`, `product design`, `user research`, `usability testing`, `figma`, `sketch`, `adobe xd`, `prototyping`, `design systems`, `accessibility`, `wcag`, `html`, `css`, `nn/g ux certification`.

## Mistakes to avoid

- A portfolio of final screens with no process or result.
- Revealing NDA material, real users or participants.
- A broken or password-locked link with no password given.
- Claiming team work as solo, or research never done.
