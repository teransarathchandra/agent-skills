# Role profile: product management

Load this when the person is a product manager or product owner. Follow it for intake, evidence, CV shape, keywords and screening answers.

## Titles and adjacent roles

- Common titles: Product Manager, Associate Product Manager, Senior Product Manager, Product Owner, Technical Product Manager, Group Product Manager, Product Lead.
- Realistic moves after a layoff: product owner to product manager (or back); business analyst, project manager or UX researcher to product owner; product manager to product operations or growth; PM in one domain to the same role in an adjacent domain (payments to fintech infrastructure).
- Search the person's title plus "product owner", "product lead" and "product operations". Titles differ by company: a "product owner" in one ad owns the backlog only, in another owns strategy.

## Evidence sources

- Roadmaps, PRDs, one-pagers and decision logs the person wrote (share an anonymised excerpt, never the file).
- Analytics and experiment results: activation, retention, conversion, revenue or cost change, A/B test readouts.
- Jira, Linear or Azure DevOps boards: releases shipped, backlog size, sprint goals met.
- Stakeholder and customer proof: research notes, support ticket themes, praise messages, launch announcements.
- Public: launch posts, press, a personal product teardown or blog. Private under an NDA: roadmap, revenue and user numbers, unreleased features. Describe the product generically and use ranges or percentages the employer would not mind.

## Intake questions

1. Which kind of product work is the main strength? (answer as it is: discovery, delivery and backlog, growth, platform or technical, data products)
2. Which direction do you want?
   a) **Same role and level (Recommended: strongest evidence)**
   b) Product owner to product manager, or the reverse
   c) Move in from another role (analyst, project manager, designer, engineer)
   d) Other: type it
3. What outcomes can you point to? (pick any)
   a) **A metric you moved, with before and after (Recommended: strongest proof)**
   b) Launches shipped and adopted
   c) Revenue, cost or time saved
   d) Other: type it
4. Do you hold a product certificate, such as PSPO or CSPO? (answer as it is: name and level, or none)
5. Can you share any product artefact publicly?
   a) **A redacted PRD or teardown (Recommended: safe sample)**
   b) No, everything is under an NDA, so I will describe it
   c) Other: type it

## CV shape

- Order: header, summary, skills, experience, certifications, education, optional product links.
- Summary leads with years in product, the product type (B2B SaaS, marketplace, internal platform), team and stakeholder scale, and one outcome.
- Bullets, outcome then action then how:
  - "Raised onboarding completion from 48% to 67% by redesigning the first-run flow with 3 engineers and 1 designer at Example Learning."
  - "Prioritised a 140-item backlog using impact and effort scoring, shipping 9 releases in a year for a 5-team programme."
  - "Cut support tickets about invoicing by a third after interviewing 18 customers and simplifying the invoice screen."
- Show the outcome and the person's own decision. Never invent a number.

## Keywords and credentials

- Keywords from the ad: roadmap, backlog, user stories, discovery, stakeholder management, KPIs, OKRs, A/B testing, SQL, Agile, Scrum, go-to-market, prioritisation, Jira.
- Many product ads name a certificate as a plus, and some name none. Product Owner certificates seen: PSPO (Scrum.org), CSPO (Scrum Alliance). IIBA's Certificate in Product Ownership Analysis (CPOA) is listed by IIBA. These are patterns, not rules; check the target ads before leading with one.
- Product experience and shipped outcomes usually weigh more than a certificate.

## Portfolio and work samples

- Not usually expected. Some ads ask for a case study or a product teardown, and take-home exercises are common late in hiring.
- A good sample is a short write-up: problem, evidence, options considered, decision, result. Use a public product, or redact names and numbers.

## Screening questions

- "Years of product management experience": count roles with real ownership of a product or backlog; do not count project support. Product owner years and product manager years may be asked separately.
- "Have you used Jira, SQL, Figma?": answer from `experience.items`; say how it was used.
- "Do you hold PSPO or CSPO?": answer from the bank, true or false; "in progress" only if booked.
- "Experience with B2B, B2C or payments?": answer from the bank domain list, not from interest.

## Answer-bank experience keys

Collect lowercase keys with years or true/false: `product management`, `product ownership`, `roadmapping`, `backlog management`, `user research`, `a/b testing`, `sql`, `jira`, `confluence`, `figma`, `amplitude`, `google analytics`, `agile`, `scrum`, `okrs`, `pspo`, `cspo`.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Listing responsibilities ("managed the backlog") with no outcome.
- Claiming a metric the whole team moved as the person's alone.
- Presenting a project manager's delivery work as product strategy.
- Leaking real roadmap, revenue or user figures covered by an NDA.
