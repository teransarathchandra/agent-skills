# Interview preparation

Interviewers ask about every line on the CV. Prepare from the same evidence the CV was built from, so the candidate tells the same true story in the room.

## STAR stories

For each bullet an interviewer is likely to pick (the first two bullets of each recent role, every number, every technology the ad names), write a short story:

- **Situation:** the product and the problem, in one or two sentences. Generic client names, as on the CV.
- **Task:** what the candidate was responsible for.
- **Action:** what the candidate did, in the first person singular. Separate it from what the team did.
- **Result:** the outcome, with the same number as the CV and where it came from.

Keep each to about 150 words, so it takes under two minutes to say. Prepare six to eight; one story usually answers several behavioural questions (conflict, failure, deadline, ambiguity, leadership).

Mark any story without a source "to verify" and ask the candidate before they rely on it.

## Gaps

For every gap the cover letter named, prepare one honest answer: what the candidate has done that is closest, and how they would close the gap in the first months. Never claim experience the evidence does not show.

## Questions to ask the employer

Prepare three or four that the ad does not answer, for example how the team ships and reviews code, what the first three months look like, how on-call works, and, for candidates abroad, how the visa or contractor arrangement would work in practice.

## Logistics

- Work out the interview time in both time zones on the interview date (daylight saving moves it).
- Test the video tool, camera and microphone the day before.
- Keep the CV version that was sent, the letter and the ad text side by side.

## Output

One file per job in the private working folder: `Interview_[EMPLOYER].md`, with the stories, gap answers and questions. It holds no salary, ID or address data.
